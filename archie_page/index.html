ASSISTANTS, NOT ARCHITECTS

Archie: explicit reasoning
for systems design.

Archie helps architects choose systems that work together and meet their priorities. It combines expert-reviewed specifications with constraint-based optimization to find feasible designs and explain the choices behind them.

LLMs are not yet reliable architects. They can recommend convincing components while missing hardware constraints or the consequences of changing objectives. Archie makes those requirements and trade-offs explicit so they can be checked together.

The two AI conversations below show why Archie is needed: follow the requirements, inspect the mistakes, and see how explicit constraints and priorities change the result.

ANALYSIS / BFC AND FEEDBACK DELAY

When is BFC worth the switch cost?

BFC’s central motivation is very high bandwidth with messages small relative to the bandwidth-delay product (BDP). If most messages fit well within one round trip, end-to-end congestion control reacts too late to shape them.

Hop-by-hop feedback reacts sooner. Its switch resource and deployment costs need justification through a workload that benefits from that faster control, especially with bursty traffic and limited buffers.

The AI supplies no such justification. The prompt gives long-flow tags, but no RTT or message-size distribution. A link-speed cutoff alone cannot settle the choice.

ANALYSIS / PACKET SPRAYING AND NIC MEMORY

Packet spraying moves complexity to the receiver.

Sending successive packets along different paths can introduce significant reordering. A receiver that restores order before delivery must hold later packets while earlier ones are still in flight.

At high rates, that can require a substantial reorder buffer at the NIC. Capacity depends on traffic rate, path-delay differences, and concurrent flows; RDMA support alone does not establish that the receiver can cope.

The AI chooses PacketSpray and RDMA without explaining the receiver design or checking this resource requirement. Archie makes that assumption explicit and checks it against the catalog.

ARCHIE / RECORDED RESULT

Make the assumptions inspectable.

Archie’s result selects DCQCN instead of BFC and PLB instead of PacketSpray, retaining RDMA. It selects NetChannel for the CPU scheduler by applying the architect’s experience-based rankings within the stated objective priorities and feasibility constraints.

The recorded explanation forces BFC into the configuration and exposes the model’s bandwidth policy. Architects and experts can inspect and challenge that policy, then solve again.

The table shows a precomputed result for this design problem. Its reported hardware cost is 11,760 catalog units; this webpage does not run the solver.

02 / WHAT THESE CONVERSATIONS SHOW

Plausible recommendations
are not enough.

The first answer provides no resource or cost justification for its choices, which also conflict with Archie’s encoded hardware policies. The second differs from the paper’s latency-first choice. These examples motivate explicit reasoning and validation; they do not measure how often LLMs fail.

LLMs remain useful assistants. They can help interpret documentation and express a design problem. Archie gives architects and system experts a way to make the underlying knowledge explicit, check combinations, and inspect why an alternative was rejected.

03 / THE ROLE FOR ARCHIE

Keep human expertise.
Make the reasoning explicit.

These conversations show why plausible recommendations need a separate reasoning process. Archie combines expert-reviewed specifications with the architect’s constraints and objectives, then uses Z3 to check and optimize the design.

How Archie turns knowledge into a design

System expert

Encode and audit requirements, compatibility rules, resource use, and conditional preferences.

Architect

Supply workload, topology, hardware inventory, deployment constraints, and objective priorities.

Archie: formulate and solve

Combine both inputs into a constraint model. Check feasibility and optimize objectives in priority order using Z3.

Inspect the design and its explanation

Review the selected systems and hardware, or the conflicting constraints. Refine the inputs and validate the deployment.

LLMs can assist with drafting specifications and presenting results. Experts review the specifications; the solver reasons over the encoded model.

01

Make knowledge explicit.

Experts encode hardware prerequisites, resource limits, and incompatibilities. Architects contribute conditional comparisons grounded in their experience and experiments. LLMs can help draft; humans audit the rules.

RDMA ⇒ NIC.RDMA = true
02

Optimize what matters.

Architects supply workloads, topology, inventory, and ordered objectives. Z3 searches for a design that respects those constraints and priorities.

latency → deployment → cost
03

Ask “why not?”

Try an alternative choice. Archie can expose the trade-off or identify conflicting constraints in an UNSAT core, with expert-provided labels.

“Why wasn’t BFC selected?”

The model is deliberately lightweight: Archie reasons over encoded rules and qualitative preferences. It does not predict exact latency or replace deployment validation.

System expert: encode the two requirements

These policy definitions implement the RDMA and PacketSpray requirements shown above. They belong in the existing systems/rdma.py and systems/packetspray.py modules. Together with the hardware catalog, they let the solver identify the incompatible combination within this model. The value 20 reproduces the artifact; it is not a universal NIC capacity requirement.

ARCHIE PYTHON API System policy definitions
from src.archie import *

# systems/rdma.py: every compute must support RDMA.
def rdma_apply(workload):
    computes = workload.topology.get_children(
        device_type=DEVICE_TYPE.COMPUTE)
    return And(*[
        host.configuration["RDMA"]
        for host in computes])

RDMA = System(
    "RDMA", transport, rdma_apply,
    objectives=[latency, throughput,
                ease_of_deployment])

# systems/packetspray.py: enforce the buffer limit.
def packetspray_apply(workload):
    computes = workload.topology.get_children(
        device_type=DEVICE_TYPE.COMPUTE)
    return And(*[
        host.configuration["NIC_Reorder_Buffer"] > 20
        for host in computes])

PACKETSPRAY = System(
    "PacketSpray", load_balancer, packetspray_apply,
    objectives=[load_balancing])
